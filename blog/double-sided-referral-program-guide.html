Double-Sided Referral Programs: A Plain-English Setup Guide

Why rewarding both the advocate and the friend works, and how to set the offer, the timing and the rules.

Published 10 November 2026

A double-sided referral program rewards two people: the customer who makes the recommendation and the friend who acts on it. It's one of the most common structures because it makes the ask feel like a gift rather than a favour.

Why it works

When an advocate can say “use my link and you'll get 20% off your first visit,” they're helping their friend, not just the business. The friend has a reason to act now, and the advocate has a reason to share.

Step 1: Set the friend's offer

Make it simple and immediate: a first-visit discount, a free add-on, or credit on the first order. Avoid complicated conditions. Friends should understand the offer at a glance.

Step 2: Set the advocate's reward

Pay it only after the friend's purchase is confirmed. Choose a reward that brings the advocate back too; our reward guide walks through the options.

Step 3: Choose when to ask

The best moment is right after a good experience: at checkout, when a service is finished, or when an order arrives. A follow-up a few days later catches people who meant to share but forgot. Respect quiet hours and only message people who have opted in.

Step 4: Make claiming effortless

Friends should be able to claim with a name and phone number on a simple page, with no app and no password. Every extra step loses people.

Step 5: Confirm, then reward

Decide how a referred sale is confirmed: a staff check at the till, a unique code, or the online order itself. Reward promptly once it's confirmed; a fast thank-you encourages the next referral.

Step 6: Review monthly

Track referred customers, referred revenue and the cost per referred customer. If people join but don't share, improve the friend's offer. If friends claim but don't buy, simplify the claim step.
