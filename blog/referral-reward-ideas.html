How to Choose a Referral Reward Customers Actually Want

Store credit, cash or a free service? A practical framework for picking rewards that motivate sharing without eating your margin.

Published 10 November 2026

The reward is the part of a referral program people notice first, and the part most businesses get wrong. Too small and nobody bothers. Too generous and the program quietly loses money. Here's a simple way to pick one that works.

Start from what a new customer is worth

Before choosing a reward, estimate what a typical new customer spends with you in their first few months, then decide what share of that you're comfortable giving away to win them. That number is your reward budget per referred customer. Everything else is about how you spend it.

Match the reward to the relationship

  • Store credit or a discount suits businesses people visit repeatedly, like salons, cafés and gyms. It brings the advocate back, and the real cost to you is lower than its face value.
  • A free service or product feels generous and personal, and works well where you have spare capacity.
  • Points fit businesses with an existing loyalty habit.
  • Cash or gift cards are the strongest motivators, but they cost exactly what they say. Keep them for high-value sales.
  • Commission suits online products and affiliates who refer often.

Reward both sides

A welcome offer for the friend gives the advocate something to share that isn't just a favour to you. We cover this in detail in our double-sided program guide.

Protect the budget

Pay only after a confirmed purchase, hold rewards until your refund window has passed, and set a monthly ceiling. Tiered rewards, where the reward grows after the third or fifth referral, keep your best advocates engaged without raising the cost of every single referral.
